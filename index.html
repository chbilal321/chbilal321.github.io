From predicting whether a drug crosses the blood–brain barrier to generating molecules that should.

I'm a computational researcher with more than six years in machine-learning-driven drug discovery, structural bioinformatics and precision medicine. During my PhD at Chung-Ang University I built LightBBB and LogBB_Pred, two open prediction servers now used by more than 2,000 researchers worldwide.

Today, as an Alberta Innovates Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Alberta, I develop generative models, transformers and graph neural networks for de novo design of CNS-penetrant compounds, and an AI platform that nominates new drug targets from multi-omics data. I work closely with medicinal chemists and biochemists, and I supervise Master's and PhD students.

Research

Four ideas I keep returning to, each drawn live in your browser.

Molecular dynamics

Docking gives a snapshot; dynamics shows whether the pose survives. I use GROMACS and AMBER for conformational sampling, binding-pathway analysis and free-energy estimates — validating predictions before anyone spends time at the bench.

  • GROMACS
  • AMBER
  • Free energy
  • Allosteric sites
  • GPCRs

Generative AI for molecules

Chemical libraries only cover a sliver of drug-like space. I build diffusion models, VAEs and reinforcement-learning pipelines that generate new compounds from a protein sequence and optimise them for potency, selectivity, ADMET safety and brain penetration at the same time.

  • Diffusion models
  • VAEs
  • Reinforcement learning
  • Transformers
  • GNNs
  • PROTACs

ADMET classifiers

Most drug candidates fail on absorption, metabolism or toxicity rather than potency. I build AutoML and ensemble classifiers for BBB permeability, CYP inhibition, hERG, liver injury and mutagenicity, so bad candidates are dropped early and cheaply.

  • AutoML
  • LightGBM
  • XGBoost
  • Ensembles
  • RDKit

AI-driven target discovery

Before designing a molecule you need the right target. I integrate multi-omics data with protein–protein interaction networks to nominate and rank druggable targets — currently for gastric and intestinal cancer — and to stratify patients for precision medicine.

  • Multi-omics
  • PPI networks
  • Druggability
  • Precision medicine

Tools anyone can use

Two web servers from my PhD, and open code from my current work.

Web server · classification

LightBBB

Predicts whether a compound penetrates the blood–brain barrier (BBB+ / BBB−), using a gradient-boosted LightGBM model.

Bioinformatics, 2021 · 214 citations

Open LightBBB Web server · regression

LogBB_Pred

Goes one step further and predicts the quantitative logBB value — how much of a compound reaches the brain compared with the blood.

Bioinformatics, 2023 · 70 citations

Open LogBB_Pred

Multi-endpoint toxicity prediction

AI platform for early-stage drug toxicity: weighted ensembles of Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost for Ames mutagenicity, carcinogenicity, liver injury (DILI), hERG and hematotoxicity.

  • Ames
  • Carcinogenicity
  • DILI
  • hERG
  • Hematotoxicity

AutoML for ADMET prediction

Code and data for the J. Chem. Inf. Model. 2025 study: hyperparameter-optimised AutoML models for eleven ADMET endpoints, trainable on your own SMILES data.
